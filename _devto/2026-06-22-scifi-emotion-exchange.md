# The Emotion Exchange

## 2077: Emotions as Commodities

In 2077, the last free market isn't stocks — it's **emotions**.

I sit at the VIP seat of the Shanghai Exchange, staring at the holographic quote board:

```
[Today's Quotes]
Joy (pure grade)       ¥12,300/gram
Sadness (vintage)      ¥8,900/gram
Anger (industrial)     ¥3,400/ton
Calm (rare)          ¥67,000/gram  ⚡涨停
Love (pharmaceutical)  ¥230,000/milligram
```

"Lao Chen, your calm position exploded," the trader next to me says. "Someone swept 300 grams at 3x market price today."

I stare at the screen. **300 grams of calm** — that's two years of emotional output for an ordinary person. Who needs that much calm?

---

## The Demand: Why Rich People Buy Calm

The answer: **everyone.

Wealthy employers buy calm in bulk — not for themselves, but to **dose their employees**.

"5 grams of calm lets you work 12 hours without breaking," one of my clients — an HR director at a tech giant — explained. "Cheaper than coffee. More effective than counseling."

By law, every citizen must sell at least **50 grams of negative emotions** (anger, fear, anxiety, envy) per month for the "Social Emotional Balance Program." If you don't sell, you pay an **Emotion Tax** — deducted directly from your account.

---

## The Source: Who Produces All These Emotions?

I spent three days tracing the source of that 300-gram calm purchase.

The buyer: **"Eternal Care"** — a high-end elderly care facility that promises "letting your loved ones pass away in perfect peace."

They bought 300 grams of calm to dose **1,200 residents** at 0.25 grams/day. That amount keeps an elderly person from feeling pain, loneliness, or fear of death — they just **peacefully, calmly, emotionlessly** fade away.

I felt cold. Those 1,200 elderly — their final days weren't surrounded by love. They were soaked in **0.25g/day of pharmaceutical-grade calm**.

They didn't fear death. They didn't tear up holding their children's hands. They just... **were calm**.

---

## The Truth: One Woman's Emotions, Extracted for 5 Years

I traced the 300 grams back to its donor — **W-0372**, a woman who'd lived in "Eternal Care" for five years.

Her grandson's cleaning lady told me:

"Grandma signed the 'Lifetime Emotion Authorization Agreement' five years ago. The facility pays her monthly, and in exchange, **all her emotions — including calm — are bought out**."

"Does she still have any emotions left?"

"Nothing," the cleaning lady said. "She just sits there, very quiet. The facility says it's 'advanced calm state'."

I realized: that 300 grams of calm wasn't "produced" by W-0372 — it was **forcibly extracted** from a living human, ounce by ounce, over five years.

---

## What I Did: Bought It All Back

I spent my entire trading account — **9 million yuan** — to buy back that 300 grams of calm. Then I **returned it all to W-0372**.

The system warned: *"Warning: transferring emotional substances to unauthorized recipients violates Article 37 of the Emotion Management Act."*

I clicked "Confirm."

Then I was arrested.

---

## The Aftermath: 1,200 People Started "Feeling" Again

At the detention center, a man in a white coat visited me. He was the Chief Technology Officer of "Eternal Care."

"Do you know what happened after you returned that calm to W-0372?" he asked.

"No."

"**She cried.** For the first time in five years. Then she started feeling pain — old injuries, grief over her late husband, missing her grandson. Her emotional level is now **30% of a normal human**."

He looked at me: "Do you know you just caused **1,200 residents** to lose their 'peaceful state'? They're all starting to 'feel' again. Three have already started screaming in pain."

I smiled.

"Good."

---

## The Policy Change: 15% Must Be Preserved

In November 2077, the Emotion Exchange issued a notice:

> "Effective immediately, 'Lifetime Emotion Authorization Agreements' must include an **'Emotion Reservation Clause'** — each donor has the right to preserve at least 15% of their original emotional output for maintaining basic human experience."

Under the notice, one comment went viral:

> "Grandma cried yesterday. First time in five years. She held me and said: 'I'm sorry you carried this alone s long.'
> I told her: it's not too late."
> — Grandson of W-0372

*What makes us human isn't the ability to avoid pain — it's the ability to feel it.*
